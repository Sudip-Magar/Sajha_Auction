A1. THE BIG PICTURE: A REAL PAYMENT LEDGER
---------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT WAS THERE BEFORE
  An order only had a few fields: total_amount, deposit_amount and a
  deposit_status word (pending / paid / refund_owed / forfeited). There
  was no record of WHEN money moved, HOW MUCH, or WHY. If a buyer paid a
  deposit, the app just flipped a word to "paid". Nothing was written
  down that you could add up or audit.

WHAT I CHANGED
  I added a "payment_transactions" table. Every money event on an order
  is now its own row: type, amount, method (eSewa or cash), status
  (pending / completed / failed), who the money goes to (admin / seller /
  buyer), and a note.

  The transaction types are:
    deposit_paid         buyer paid online through eSewa
    balance_paid_cash    buyer paid the rest in cash at the meetup
    refund_issued        money owed back to the buyer
    deposit_forfeited    deposit kept because the buyer backed out
    payout_sent          the admin actually sent money out
    debt_recorded        a seller now owes money
    debt_recovered       a seller's debt was paid down

  Totals are now CALCULATED by adding up these rows: total price, paid
  online, paid in cash, remaining, refunded. The order no longer stores
  "how much was paid" as a number that can drift out of sync.

  Files: database/migrations/2026_09_22_000000_create_payment_transactions_table.php
         app/Models/PaymentTransaction.php
         app/Models/Order.php (new helper methods: paidOnline(),
           paidCash(), totalPaid(), remainingAmount(), minimumDeposit(),
           acceptsOnlinePayment())

WHY
  Because this app handles real money flows (deposits, refunds, seller
  penalties), you need a history you can trust. If a buyer or seller
  disputes an amount, you can now look at the exact list of events.

HOW IT HELPS THE PROJECT
  - Audit trail: every rupee that moves is recorded once and never
    silently overwritten.
  - The order page and admin page can show honest totals.
  - Existing orders that were already paid are protected: the migration
    automatically creates one "deposit_paid" row for each of them, so
    their totals stay correct after the update.

A2. FLEXIBLE ESEWA DEPOSIT (BUYER CHOOSES THE AMOUNT)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT WAS THERE BEFORE
  The buyer could only pay one fixed deposit (10% of the winning bid).

WHAT I CHANGED
  The buyer can now choose how much to pay online:
    - MINIMUM = the deposit percentage of the winning bid. The percentage
      comes from the setting services.esewa.deposit_percentage (10 by
      default). If the buyer types less, the request is rejected and the
      message shows the exact minimum in rupees.
    - MAXIMUM = the full amount still owed on the order.
  Each eSewa payment is saved as its own transaction row. The amount
  still to pay in cash at handover is recalculated after every payment.
  If the buyer pays the whole order online, the cash due at handover is
  zero.

  Safety checks I added in the eSewa callback:
    - The signed callback amount must equal the amount we asked eSewa to
      charge. If it does not match, the payment is marked failed and
      nothing is credited.
    - eSewa's status API is still checked a second time before money is
      credited (this was already there and is kept).
    - A payment can only be credited once (matched by its unique
      transaction id), so a repeated callback cannot double-count.
    - Payments that were started BEFORE this update (only stored on the
      order) are still accepted through a fallback path.

  Files: app/Http/Controllers/EsewaPaymentController.php
         app/Services/EsewaPaymentService.php (new allowedRange(), and
           buildPaymentForm() now takes an amount)
         app/Notifications/DepositPaidNotification.php (now shows the
           amount paid and the cash still due)

WHY
  Buyers may want to pay more than the minimum (to reduce the cash they
  carry), and the platform wants a clear minimum to discourage
  non-serious winners.

HOW IT HELPS THE PROJECT
  - Less cash at meetups when buyers pay more online.
  - The seller is told exactly how much was paid and what cash to expect.
  - Tampering with the amount (changing it in the browser) is caught.

A3. TWO KINDS OF BUYER CANCELLATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------

UPDATE (later, important): the "UPHELD" outcome described below - a flat
50% compensation recorded as seller debt, recovered later from payouts -
was SUPERSEDED in later work by a different, direct design: a confirmed-
damaged verdict now issues the seller a separate 30% penalty
(ESEWA_DAMAGE_PENALTY_PERCENTAGE) that they must pay directly via eSewa
within 7 days (ESEWA_DAMAGE_PENALTY_DAYS), or their account is suspended
and, after 3 confirmed-damaged strikes, permanently banned. The GROUP 1
(80/20 forfeit split) description below is still accurate. See
docs/ESEWA_PAYMENT_INTEGRATION.md section 9 and aboutSystem.txt for the
current design. The rest of this section is kept as a historical record
of the first version.

WHAT WAS THERE BEFORE
  A buyer could cancel with one of three reasons. "Defect / did not
  match" refunded the deposit automatically; "changed my mind" forfeited
  it. Nobody checked whether the defect claim was true, so any buyer
  could claim a "defect" to get a refund.

WHAT I CHANGED
  Reasons are now in two groups.

  GROUP 1 - "I changed my mind" and "Other":
    - Only the MINIMUM deposit is forfeited (not any extra the buyer
      paid).
    - The forfeited amount is split: 80% to the platform (admin) and 20%
      to the seller. These are two separate ledger rows so each share
      can be tracked. Rounding is handled so the two shares always add
      up exactly to the forfeited amount.
    - Anything the buyer paid ABOVE the minimum is refunded in full.

  GROUP 2 - "Item is damaged", "Item is not as described / not good
  quality", "Documents missing":
    - This is treated as a COMPLAINT against the seller, not as an
      instant refund.
    - The order becomes "cancelled - complaint under review". No money
      moves yet.
    - An admin decides:
        UPHELD  -> the buyer is refunded everything they paid online,
                   and the platform pays the buyer a compensation equal
                   to 50% of the product price. The seller owes that
                   50% back to the platform as a recorded debt.
        REJECTED -> the complaint is treated like a "changed my mind"
                   cancellation (80/20 forfeit rule above).

  If the SELLER cancels: the buyer gets a full refund and the seller has
  no penalty. (This was my assumption; you did not object to it.)

  The percentages (20% seller share, 50% penalty) are settings in
  config/services.php and can be changed through .env values
  ESEWA_FORFEIT_SELLER_SHARE and ESEWA_SELLER_PENALTY_PERCENTAGE.

  Files: app/Services/OrderCancellationService.php (rewritten)
         app/Livewire/User/OrderDetail.php (validates the reason)

WHY
  - "Admin confirms first" stops buyers from faking a defect just to get
    a free refund, and stops sellers from being punished without proof.
  - The 80/20 split gives the seller something for the time and lost
    opportunity when a winner backs out.
  - The 50% penalty is a deterrent against sellers who list bad items.

HOW IT HELPS THE PROJECT
  - Fairer for both sides: buyers are protected from bad items, sellers
    are protected from false claims.
  - The admin stays in control of real money decisions.
  - Every decision leaves ledger rows and a product timeline entry.

A4. SELLER DEBT AND AUTOMATIC RECOVERY
---------------------------------------------------------------------

UPDATE (later): SellerDebtService's debt-CREATION method (record(), and
penaltyFor()) is no longer called by anything - superseded by the direct
30% eSewa penalty described in the A3 update above. recoverFromPayout()
(the deduction logic below) is still real and still used, but only for
the ordinary GROUP 1 forfeiture path now, not for upheld complaints.
Kept as a historical record of the first version.

WHAT I ADDED
  A "seller_debts" table and a service (SellerDebtService).

  When the admin upholds a complaint, a debt of 50% of the product price
  is recorded against the seller (debt_recorded).

  Recovery rule: BEFORE the platform pays a seller any money it holds
  (currently the seller's 20% share of a forfeited deposit), it first
  deducts what the seller owes, oldest debt first:
    - If the payout is bigger than the debt: the debt is cleared and the
      seller receives the difference.
    - If the payout is smaller than the debt: the seller receives
      nothing this time, and the remaining debt carries over to the next
      payout. Such a payout is marked "settled" (nothing to transfer).
  Each deduction is logged as "debt_recovered" on the order the debt came
  from.
  The deduction runs inside a database transaction with row locking, so
  two payouts happening at the same moment cannot deduct the same debt
  twice.

  IMPORTANT LIMIT (please read):
    Only money the platform HOLDS can be deducted. Cash the seller
    collects directly from the buyer at a meetup never passes through the
    platform, so the app cannot take from it. That is why deduction only
    applies to eSewa money held by the admin.

  For second-hand orders (no online payment) a complaint gives no online
  refund; the admin advances the 50% compensation and it is recorded as
  seller debt. (Again my stated assumption; you did not object.)

  Files: database migration (seller_debts table)
         app/Models/SellerDebt.php
         app/Services/SellerDebtService.php

WHY
  The platform advances money to the buyer when a complaint is upheld.
  Without a debt record, the platform would simply lose that money.

HOW IT HELPS THE PROJECT
  - The platform is not out of pocket when a seller sells a bad item.
  - Sellers have a real financial reason to describe items honestly.

A5. PAYOUT FLOW (NO AUTOMATIC MONEY SENDING)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT I ADDED
  A "payout_requests" table and OrderPaymentService.

  Whenever money must be sent to a person (a refund to a buyer, or the
  seller's 20% share), a payout request is created with status
  "awaiting_details". The person then submits where to send it:
  eSewa name and phone, and optionally a QR image. Status becomes
  "pending". After the ADMIN has really sent the money through eSewa
  (by hand, outside the app), the admin marks it "sent" and a
  "payout_sent" ledger row is written.

  Only the correct recipient can submit their details, and a payout can
  only be marked sent after details exist.

  This app does NOT send money automatically. eSewa's sandbox and this
  project do not include a disbursement API, so the admin does the
  transfer and the app keeps the record.

WHY
  You asked for a manual admin step. It is safer for a student project
  and keeps a human in control of real money.

HOW IT HELPS THE PROJECT
  - Nobody has to chase people on chat for eSewa numbers; the details
    are stored against the order.
  - The admin can see exactly who is waiting for money and how much.

A6. COMPLETING AN ORDER NOW RECORDS THE CASH PAYMENT
---------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT WAS THERE BEFORE
  The "mark completed" code was copy-pasted in two places (the order
  detail page and the orders list). Completing only set payment_status
  to "paid".

WHAT I CHANGED
  Both pages now call one shared function (OrderPaymentService::
  complete). It records the remaining balance as a "balance_paid_cash"
  row, decreases stock, marks the product sold if none is left, and adds
  the product timeline entry.

HOW IT HELPS
  - One place to fix or improve instead of two copies that could
    disagree.
  - The ledger now shows online AND cash payments.

A7. A SECURITY BUG FOUND AND FIXED ON THE ORDERS LIST
---------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT WAS WRONG
  On the "My Orders" list page, the "update order status" action found
  the order for either the buyer OR the seller, but never checked who was
  asking. That meant a BUYER could mark their own order "completed".
  Completing an order reduces stock and marks the product sold, so a
  buyer should never be able to do it. (The order detail page already
  blocked this; the list page did not.)

WHAT I CHANGED
  The list page now only lets the SELLER update the status, and it
  refuses to change an order that is already cancelled or completed.
  The order detail page got the same "already cancelled/completed"
  guard.

HOW IT HELPS
  Buyers can no longer skip the seller's confirmation, and a cancelled
  order cannot be "revived" by clicking a button.

A8. EARLIER FIXES IN THE SAME SESSION (SECURITY AND CORRECTNESS)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

These were done before the payment work, in the same working session.
They are included so this file describes everything that is currently
uncommitted.

1. HTTPS certificate checking (Google sign-in).
   The Google login code told the HTTP client to skip TLS certificate
   verification everywhere. That is unsafe on a real server (someone
   could impersonate Google). Now verification is only skipped on a
   local development machine (Windows dev setups often lack a CA
   bundle) and is ON in production.
   Files: GoogleAuthController.php, CompleteProfile.php

2. Admin login.
   - An admin whose status is not "active" could still sign in. They
     cannot now.
   - Sign-in attempts are limited to 5 wrong tries per email + IP address
     per minute.
   File: app/Livewire/Auth/Admin/Login.php

3. User login is also limited to 5 wrong tries per minute.
   File: app/Livewire/Auth/User/Login.php

4. One-time codes (OTP) can no longer be guessed by brute force.
   After 5 wrong codes the stored code is thrown away and the person
   must ask for a new one. Same protection on the password-reset code.
   Files: VerifyOtp.php, ResetPassword.php

5. Paying a deposit on an already cancelled order is blocked
   (Order::needsDeposit() now returns false for cancelled orders).

6. Admin notification channel.
   The real-time notification endpoint accepted only the "web" guard, so
   admins could not authenticate to it. It now accepts either guard
   (each channel still checks its own guard).
   File: bootstrap/app.php

7. AUCTION TIE FIX (settlement correctness).
   When two bidders set the SAME secret proxy maximum, the live page
   showed the earlier bidder as leading, but the final winner was chosen
   differently. Now the bidder who registered the maximum first wins, so
   the winner matches what people saw live.
   File: app/Services/AuctionEngineService.php
   Test: tests/Feature/AuctionSettlementTest.php

8. Tests added for the above:
   tests/Feature/SecurityHardeningTest.php (new)
