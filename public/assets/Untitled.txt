
Stage 0: who is allowed to auction

- Selling: a user must be is_seller. For an auction listing they also need is_auction_allowed, which an admin grants after reviewing their Join Auction application (ManageProduct::mount).
- Bidding: bidders need the same is_auction_allowed flag. It is checked in the UI and again inside processBid.

Seller helpers:
- Estimated value: AuctionValuationService computes retail_price × (1 − depreciation) × condition factor. Depreciation is a step table by age in months: under 6 months is 10%, then 18%, 30%, 45%, 60% and 70%, capped at 75% from 60 months. The condition factors are 1.05 for new or like-new, 1.00 for lightly-used or refurbished, and 0.90 for well-used.
- Suggested starting price: 80% of the estimated value. It is a one-click suggestion only, never enforced.
- Suggested reserve (Algorithm 3a, Myerson): the average of the retail price and the starting bid, floored at the starting bid. Also a suggestion.

What gets saved: a products row with status=pending, an auctions row with status=pending and current_price=starting_bid, and a traditional_auctions row holding the bid rules. Admins are notified.

Admin approval: Product::approveListing() sets the product to approval_status=approved (replacing the old is_approved boolean), status=active and expires_at=+30 days, and flips the auction to status=active. Approved products can no longer be edited by the seller. Admins can also reject a listing (approval_status=rejected) or send it back for correction (approval_status=correction, with a remarks note the seller must address before resubmitting).

Stage 2: live bidding (AuctionDetail → AuctionEngineService::processBid)

An auction is live when status=active, the start time has passed, and the effective end time has not (Auction::isLive). The effective end time is extended_end_time ?? end_time.

Every bid runs inside a DB transaction with a row lock on the auction (lockForUpdate), so two simultaneous bids are handled one at a time.

Algorithm 2a: dynamic step increment. The minimum step depends on the current price, and the seller's min_bid_increment can only raise it:

┌────────────────┬───────┐
│ Current price  │ Step  │
├────────────────┼───────┤
│ under 1,000    │ 50    │
├────────────────┼───────┤
│ under 5,000    │ 100   │
├────────────────┼───────┤
│ under 20,000   │ 250   │
├────────────────┼───────┤
│ under 50,000   │ 500   │
├────────────────┼───────┤
│ 50,000 or more │ 1,000 │
└────────────────┴───────┘

The minimum next bid is current_price + max(step, custom increment). Even the first bid must be at least starting_bid + step, not the starting bid itself.

Algorithm 2b: proxy bidding. A bidder can enter a visible bid plus a secret maximum. The engine then does the following:
1. It records the bid, and ranks each bidder by their highest maximum. Ties go to whoever registered that maximum first.
2. With one bidder, the price is just their bid, but never below the starting bid.
3. With two or more bidders, it applies p* = min(m1, m2 + step(m2)), where m1 is the leader's maximum and m2 is the runner-up's. The leader pays just above the runner-up's ceiling, never above their own.
4. If the leader is a proxy bidder and the price moved, it inserts an automatic bid row for them at the new price. This keeps the bid history matching the displayed price.

Example, with a starting bid of 1,000 and a step of 100:
1. A bids 1,100 with a maximum of 2,000. The price is 1,100.
2. B bids 1,200. A's maximum is 2,000 and B's is 1,200, so p* = min(2000, 1200+100) = 1,300. The price becomes 1,300, an automatic bid is recorded for A, and A still leads.
3. B bids 2,100. B's maximum is now higher, so p* = min(2100, 2000+100) = 2,100. B leads and A gets an outbid notification.

Anti-sniping. If a bid lands within timer_reset_seconds of the deadline, extended_end_time is pushed to now plus that window. It only ever moves later, never earlier.

Broadcasts. After the transaction commits, AuctionBidPlaced is broadcast on auctions.{id} so every viewer's page refreshes. A leader change also sends OutbidNotification to the previous leader.

Algorithm 3a: suggested bid (advisory). A bidder can type their private valuation v, and the UI suggests ((n−1)/n) × v, where n is the number of distinct bidders (minimum 2). It has no effect on the engine.

Stage 3: how the winner is decided (determineWinner, Algorithm 1)

When it runs. There are three triggers:
- The scheduler (routes/console.php) runs every minute. It needs schedule:work or a cron entry to be running.
- Anyone opening or rendering the auction page.
- Anyone placing a bid.

All three call checkAndFinalizeIfExpired, which does nothing until the effective end time has actually passed, so an extension is respected.

The rule:
1. Keep only bids where bid_amount >= reserve_price. The comparison is on the visible bid amount, never on a secret proxy ceiling.
2. If none qualify, the status becomes ended_unsold.
3. Otherwise the highest bid_amount wins. If tied, the earliest submission wins. The status becomes completed, and the winner and winning price are recorded.
4. The product is marked sold.

What the winner gets. An Order is created automatically:
- Status: pending.
- Handover: always meetup with cash on meetup, at the product's meetup location or, failing that, its general location.
- Deposit: 10% of the winning price (configurable), starting as deposit_status=pending.
- Notifications: the winner receives an AuctionWonNotification and an AuctionWonMail.

Stage 4: handover ("delivery")

There is no courier for auctions. It is a face-to-face handover, run from the order detail page (OrderDetail):
1. Deposit: the order is created with no meetup details yet - the winner sets (or later edits) the meetup location, date and time from the order page (a dedicated "Schedule Meetup" action; this didn't exist when this note was first written, and the order used to just say "they meet" with no scheduling step at all). The winner also pays a deposit through eSewa - the amount is a buyer-chosen amount within a minimum/maximum range (minimum = the deposit percentage of the winning price, maximum = the full remaining balance), not a fixed 10%. This is sandbox only.
2. Seller confirms: only the seller can move the order to confirmed.
3. Meetup: they meet, the buyer inspects the item, and the remaining balance is paid in cash.
4. Seller completes: only the seller can move the order to completed. This sets payment_status=paid, decrements the product quantity and logs a timeline entry.
5. Cancellation: either side can cancel, with a reason from one of two groups.
   - Group 1 (no dispute) - seller cancels for any reason, or buyer cites "changed mind"/"other": the minimum deposit is forfeited and split, by default 80% to the platform and 20% to the seller (configurable via ESEWA_FORFEIT_SELLER_SHARE), with any excess refunded (seller cancelling refunds the buyer in full instead).
   - Group 2 (a complaint) - buyer cites "item damaged", "not as described", or "documents missing": the order goes to "under review" instead of settling any money. An admin is notified and physically inspects the item, then records a verdict: confirmed damaged refunds the buyer in full, suspends the seller's account, and issues a separate 30% penalty due within 7 days via eSewa (three confirmed-damaged strikes bans the seller permanently); not damaged falls back to the Group 1 forfeit rule. A dedicated chat thread lets the buyer and an admin message each other about the complaint while it's under review.
   - In every case the product goes back to active if it was marked sold.
