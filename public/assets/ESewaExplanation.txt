1. WHAT ESEWA IS AND WHY WE USE IT (BIG PICTURE)
=====================================================================
eSewa is Nepal's digital wallet / online payment service. A website can
send a customer to eSewa's payment page, the customer pays with their
eSewa wallet, and eSewa sends the customer back to the website.

eSewa is used for TWO things in Sajha Auction:
      1. SECURING AN AUCTION WIN - the buyer pays some or all of the
         winning price online before the meetup (sections 1-7 below).
      2. A DAMAGE PENALTY - if an admin confirms a buyer's damage
         complaint, the seller must pay a separate 30% penalty directly
         to the admin through eSewa (section 8a).

Why the auction payment exists: when someone wins an auction there is a
real risk that they simply disappear and never come to the meetup. To
discourage that, the winner must pay AT LEAST a minimum deposit (a
configurable percentage of the winning price) online, and may choose to
pay MORE - up to the full price - if they prefer not to carry cash to
the meetup. Whatever is not paid online is paid in CASH at the
in-person meetup, after the buyer has inspected the item.

Everything else stays cash-only BY DESIGN:
  - Second-hand (direct-sell) checkout: in-person meetup + cash on
    meetup / handover. No online payment at all.
  - Auction balance not paid online: cash at the meetup.

The integration runs against eSewa's SANDBOX (test environment) by
default: fake money, fake accounts, but the exact same flow as
production. It is meant for the final-year project demo, not for real
money.

3. WHAT HAPPENS STEP BY STEP (THE WHOLE PAYMENT FLOW)
=====================================================================
STEP 0 - The auction ends and the winner gets an order
   determineWinner() creates an Order for the winner with:
     total_amount   = the winning price
     deposit_amount = winning price x deposit percentage / 100 (the
                      MINIMUM, not the only amount that can be paid)
     deposit_status = 'pending'
   The winner is notified (app + email).

STEP 1 - The buyer opens the order
   My Orders -> the order. The online-payment card shows the minimum
   required, an amount box the buyer can raise (up to the full
   remaining balance), and the button "Pay via eSewa". (Only the BUYER
   sees the button, and only while acceptsOnlinePayment() is true.)
   The card also explains that anything not paid online is due in cash
   at the meetup.

STEP 2 - initiate  (GET /my-orders/{order}/pay-deposit?amount=...)
   The controller checks the user is the buyer and the order still
   accepts an online payment (section 12), then works out the allowed
   range (min/max, section 8) and validates the chosen amount against
   it. The service then:
     - creates a NEW random transaction id (UUID),
     - logs a PENDING PaymentTransaction row for this amount,
     - builds the form fields and signs them (sections 4 and 5).
   The controller shows the redirect page.

STEP 3 - The redirect page auto-submits
   A hidden <form method="POST"> with all the fields is submitted by
   JavaScript to eSewa's payment page (ESEWA_FORM_URL).

STEP 4 - The buyer pays on eSewa
   The buyer logs in with their eSewa account (sandbox test account in
   development), confirms the amount and pays.

STEP 5a - eSewa sends the buyer back: SUCCESS
   eSewa redirects the browser to our success_url with a query
   parameter  ?data=<base64 text>.  Controller success():
     1. decode + verify the signature (section 6)
     2. check status inside the data is "COMPLETE"
     3. find the PENDING PaymentTransaction by its reference (uuid);
        refuse if unknown or already completed
     4. check the callback's paid amount matches the pending
        transaction's amount exactly
     5. ask eSewa's status API to confirm (section 7)
     6. only then: mark the transaction COMPLETED and deposit_status
        'paid'
     7. write a timeline entry on the product
     8. notify the SELLER (DepositPaidNotification)
     9. redirect to the order page with a green toast: "Payment
        received! The seller has been notified."

STEP 5b - eSewa sends the buyer back: FAILURE / CANCELLED
   eSewa redirects to our failure_url. Controller failure() redirects
   to the buyer's orders with a red toast: "The eSewa payment was
   cancelled or failed. You can try again from the order page." Nothing
   in the database changes except the one pending transaction row,
   which is simply left unmatched.

STEP 6 - The meetup
   Buyer and seller meet, the buyer inspects the item, pays whatever is
   still owed in cash, and the seller marks the order completed.

4. THE FORM SENT TO ESEWA (EVERY FIELD)
=====================================================================
Built in EsewaPaymentService::buildSignedForm() (shared by both the
deposit flow and the damage-penalty flow). Sent by POST.

  Field                     Value / meaning
  ------------------------  -----------------------------------------
  amount                    The amount for THIS payment, 2 decimals,
                            e.g. 3200.00
  tax_amount                "0"   (no tax)
  product_service_charge    "0"
  product_delivery_charge   "0"
  total_amount              Same as amount. This is what the customer
                            pays.
  transaction_uuid          A fresh random UUID for THIS attempt
  product_code              Our merchant code (ESEWA_PRODUCT_CODE;
                            the sandbox uses eSewa's public test code)
  success_url                The matching success route (deposit or
                            penalty) for this flow
  failure_url                The matching failure route (+ ?order= for
                            the deposit flow)
  signed_field_names        "total_amount,transaction_uuid,product_code"
                            (which fields are covered by the signature,
                            and in which order)
  signature                 The signature (section 5)

The amount is formatted like  3200.00  (dot, two decimals, no
thousands separator). The SAME formatting must be used when signing and
when calling the status API, otherwise the signatures do not match.


=====================================================================
5. THE SIGNATURE (HMAC-SHA256): HOW IT IS BUILT AND CHECKED
=====================================================================
WHAT IS A SIGNATURE?
   A signature proves two things: (1) the message really comes from
   someone who knows the shared SECRET KEY, and (2) nobody changed the
   message on the way. Our server and eSewa both know the secret key.
   A customer's browser does NOT, so the customer cannot forge it.

BUILDING IT  (private function sign() in EsewaPaymentService)
   1. Take the fields listed in signed_field_names, IN THAT ORDER.
   2. Join them as  name=value  pairs separated by commas:
        total_amount=3200.00,transaction_uuid=<the uuid>,product_code=EPAYTEST
   3. Compute HMAC-SHA256 of that text using the secret key
      (ESEWA_SECRET_KEY), as raw binary.
   4. Base64-encode the result. That text is the "signature".
   In code:  base64_encode( hash_hmac('sha256', $message, $secret, true) )

ORDER MATTERS
   total_amount, then transaction_uuid, then product_code. A different
   order or a different amount format gives a completely different
   signature and eSewa rejects the payment.

CHECKING eSewa'S RESPONSE  (decodeAndVerifyCallback)
   eSewa signs its reply the same way, but with ITS OWN list of fields
   (it tells us which one in the reply's signed_field_names). We:
     1. rebuild the message from the fields it names, in its order,
     2. sign it with OUR secret key,
     3. compare with the signature it sent using hash_equals()
        (a safe comparison that does not leak timing information).
   Different -> the reply is rejected (treated as tampered).


=====================================================================
6. THE CALLBACK: WHAT ESEWA SENDS BACK
=====================================================================
On success eSewa redirects the browser to:
      /payment/esewa/success?data=<BASE64 TEXT>
      (or /payment/esewa/penalty-success?data=... for a penalty payment)

The base64 text decodes to JSON like this (values are examples):
  {
    "transaction_code": "ABC123",
    "status": "COMPLETE",
    "total_amount": "3200.00",
    "transaction_uuid": "9b1d6c3e-....",
    "product_code": "EPAYTEST",
    "signed_field_names": "transaction_code,status,total_amount,transaction_uuid,product_code",
    "signature": "<base64 signature>"
  }
decodeAndVerifyCallback() returns null (= reject) when:
   - the text is not valid base64/JSON,
   - "signed_field_names" or "signature" is missing,
   - a field named in signed_field_names is missing,
   - the signature does not match.
On acceptance it returns the decoded array.

IMPORTANT: this arrives through the USER'S BROWSER. A person could type
any URL, so we never trust it on its own (next section).


=====================================================================
7. DOUBLE VERIFICATION (SIGNATURE + STATUS API)
=====================================================================
Three independent checks must ALL pass before a payment is marked
completed (the deposit flow also checks the amount; the same shape
applies to the penalty flow):

  CHECK 1 - Signature (section 5/6)
     Proves the reply was created by someone who knows the secret key,
     and was not changed.

  CHECK 2 - Amount match
     The callback's total_amount must match, to the cent, the amount
     stored on OUR pending PaymentTransaction (or DamagePenalty) row -
     not a value taken from the browser. A mismatch fails the
     transaction and logs a warning instead of crediting it.

  CHECK 3 - eSewa's status API (checkStatus)
     Our SERVER (not the browser) calls eSewa directly:
        GET {ESEWA_STATUS_URL}
            ?product_code=...
            &total_amount=<OUR stored amount, 2 decimals>
            &transaction_uuid=<the uuid we generated>
     eSewa answers with JSON containing "status". Only  "COMPLETE"
     passes. If the call fails (not HTTP 200) or the status is anything
     else (for example "PENDING"), the payment is NOT credited, a
     warning is written to laravel.log, and the buyer sees:
        "Payment could not be confirmed with eSewa. If money was
         deducted, contact support."

8. PAYMENT AMOUNT: HOW MUCH THE BUYER MAY PAY, AND THE LEDGER
=====================================================================
THE ALLOWED RANGE  (EsewaPaymentService::allowedRange())
   min = whatever is still needed to reach the minimum deposit (the
         configured percentage of the winning price), or Rs. 1 if the
         minimum has already been met and there is still a balance
   max = the order's full remaining balance (remainingAmount())
   If the buyer submits an amount outside this range, initiate()
   redirects back with a message explaining the minimum or maximum.

  Example:  winning price Rs 32,000, deposit percentage 10%
            minimum deposit  = Rs 3,200.00
            first payment range: Rs 3,200.00 (min) to Rs 32,000.00 (max)
            If the buyer pays exactly Rs 3,200, a SECOND payment later
            can range from Rs 1.00 (min) to the new remaining balance.

THE LEDGER  (payment_transactions table, PaymentTransaction model)
   Every movement of money on an order is one row: type (deposit_paid,
   balance_paid_cash, refund_issued, deposit_forfeited, payout_sent,
   damage_penalty_paid, plus two dead types - debt_recorded/
   debt_recovered - kept for history, see section 19), status
   (pending/completed/failed), party (who it's for/from - admin,
   buyer or seller) and an amount. Order helper methods derive
   everything from this ledger by summing COMPLETED rows of a type:
      paidOnline()      sum of deposit_paid
      paidCash()         sum of balance_paid_cash
      totalPaid()         paidOnline() + paidCash()
      remainingAmount()   total_amount - totalPaid(), floored at 0
      minimumDeposit()    deposit_amount, or the percentage recomputed
   deposit_status is still a column on the order and still drives the
   badge shown to the buyer/seller, but it is a SUMMARY the code
   updates alongside the ledger, not something computed live from it.

PAYOUTS  (payout_requests table, PayoutRequest model)
   Whenever money needs to go FROM the admin TO a buyer or seller (a
   refund, or the seller's share of a forfeited deposit), a
   PayoutRequest row is created with status AWAITING_DETAILS. The
   intended next step is: the recipient submits their eSewa name/phone
   (and optionally a QR image) via OrderPaymentService::
   submitPayoutDetails(), the admin then makes the real transfer
   outside the app and calls OrderPaymentService::markSent() to log a
   payout_sent ledger row.
   STATUS OF THIS PART: the two service methods above exist and are
   covered by tests/Feature/OrderPaymentLedgerTest.php, but NEITHER IS
   WIRED TO ANY SCREEN YET. The admin order-detail page only lists
   payout requests read-only (purpose, status, amount); there is no
   button to submit payout details or to mark one sent. A payout that
   is fully offset by seller debt (see SellerDebtService::
   recoverFromPayout()) is created directly with status SETTLED and
   needs no transfer at all.


=====================================================================
8a. THE MIRROR FLOW: A SELLER PAYING A DAMAGE PENALTY
=====================================================================
When an admin confirms a buyer's damage complaint (section 10,
OrderCancellationService::recordDamageVerdict()), DamagePenaltyService
issues the seller a separate penalty - 30% of the order total
(ESEWA_DAMAGE_PENALTY_PERCENTAGE) due within 7 days
(ESEWA_DAMAGE_PENALTY_DAYS) - and immediately suspends their seller and
auction access. Paying it off goes through eSewa exactly like an order
payment, just in the other direction (seller -> admin instead of
buyer -> admin) and against a damage_penalties row instead of an order:

  DamagePenaltyPaymentController::initiate()  - only the penalised
     seller may start this, and only while the penalty is still PENDING
     (not already paid or expired).
  EsewaPaymentService::buildPenaltyPaymentForm()  - same signed-form
     shape as buildPaymentForm(), success/failure URLs point at the
     penalty-success/penalty-failure routes instead.
  DamagePenaltyPaymentController::success()  - same signature + amount
     + status-API triple check as the deposit flow, then calls
     DamagePenaltyService::recordPayment(), which:
       - marks the penalty PAID,
       - logs a damage_penalty_paid PaymentTransaction row,
       - records a SellerDamageStrike,
       - permanently bans the seller (is_permanently_banned) on their
         3rd confirmed-damaged strike.
  A paid (or otherwise resolved) penalty does NOT automatically restore
  the seller's access - an admin must call
  DamagePenaltyService::restoreAccess() (wired to the admin order-detail
  page), which refuses if the seller has any OTHER unresolved penalty
  or has been permanently banned.
  An unpaid penalty past its due date is caught by the hourly
  expire-overdue-damage-penalties scheduled command
  (DamagePenaltyService::expireOverdue()), which deactivates the
  seller's account entirely and notifies every admin that legal/manual
  action may be needed.

The seller's own /my-penalties page (PenaltiesAndWarnings.php) lists
every penalty (pending/paid/expired) with a "Pay via eSewa" button
while pending, plus every warning ever issued. It is deliberately
reachable even after access is suspended, since that's exactly when a
seller needs to see it.

9. DEPOSIT STATUS VALUES AND EVERY TRANSITION
=====================================================================
Column: orders.deposit_status

  Value          Meaning                              Shown as
  -------------  -----------------------------------  -------------------
  not_required   Default. Ordinary second-hand         (no payment card)
                 orders have no online payment.
  pending        Auction win; the minimum has not      PENDING (amber)
                 been paid yet.
  paid           At least the minimum has been paid    PAID via eSewa (green)
                 online (ledger-tracked; more may
                 still be owed in cash).
  refund_owed    Paid online, then the order was       REFUND OWED TO BUYER
                 cancelled/resolved in a way that      (blue)
                 entitles the buyer to a refund.
  forfeited      Paid online, then the buyer            FORFEITED (red)
                 cancelled without a Group 1 (no-
                 dispute) reason; the minimum
                 deposit is forfeited.

(There is also a `failed` case in the enum for completeness; the code
never actually writes it - a failed eSewa attempt simply leaves the
prior status unchanged and the pending PaymentTransaction row sits
unmatched.)

TRANSITIONS
   (order created by an auction win)             -> pending
   pending  --minimum reached via eSewa-->           paid
   pending/paid  --failure/cancel on eSewa-->        unchanged (retry)
   paid  --seller cancels-->                         refund_owed
   paid  --buyer cancels, Group 1 reason-->           forfeited
   paid  --buyer cancels, Group 2 (complaint)-->      stays 'paid' until
                                                     an admin verdict:
                                                       confirmed damaged
                                                       -> refund_owed
                                                       not damaged
                                                       -> forfeited
   Ordinary second-hand orders stay not_required forever.

The refund itself is NOT automatic: eSewa's refund API is not
integrated. A refund becomes a PayoutRequest (section 8) that still
needs the (currently unbuilt) submit/mark-sent UI, so in practice it is
tracked in the database but paid back to the buyer OUTSIDE the app.


=====================================================================
10. CANCELLATION RULES: REFUND OR FORFEIT
=====================================================================
Code: app/Services/OrderCancellationService.php  (cancel(),
recordDamageVerdict())

Cancelling requires going through the "Cancel Order" form on the order
page. The buyer must pick a reason (OrderCancellationReason enum),
split into two groups:
   Group 1 (no dispute)
      changed_mind : "I no longer want to buy this item"
      other        : "Other reason"
   Group 2 (a complaint against the seller - isComplaintReason() is
   true for these three)
      item_damaged      : "The item is damaged"
      not_as_described  : "The item is not as described / not good quality"
      documents_missing : "Documents are missing"
An optional note can be added. The seller does not choose a reason.

What happens to money already paid online:
   Who cancels     Reason                       Result
   --------------  ---------------------------  -----------------------
   Seller           (any)                        Full refund (refund_owed)
   Buyer            Group 1 (changed_mind/other) The minimum deposit is
                                                 forfeited, split 80% to
                                                 the platform / 20% to
                                                 the seller by default
                                                 (forfeited); any excess
                                                 above the minimum is
                                                 refunded.
   Buyer            Group 2 (a complaint)        NOTHING moves yet. The
                                                 order goes to "under
                                                 review", every admin is
                                                 notified
                                                 (ComplaintFiledNotification),
                                                 and a buyer<->admin
                                                 chat thread opens.

For a Group 2 complaint, an admin later physically inspects the item
and records a verdict (recordDamageVerdict()):
   Confirmed damaged  -> the buyer is refunded everything paid online in
                        full, the seller's account is suspended, and a
                        separate 30% damage penalty is issued (section
                        8a). 3 confirmed-damaged strikes permanently
                        bans the seller.
   Not damaged        -> falls back to the Group 1 forfeit rule above.

WHY:  the payment exists to discourage a winner from backing out for no
reason. If the SELLER is at fault (confirmed damage) the buyer gets
everything back and the seller is penalised separately. If the buyer
just changed their mind, the minimum deposit is lost. A raw claim of
damage is never taken at face value - it goes through an admin verdict
first.

Other things cancel() does:
   - saves cancellation_reason_category and cancellation_note,
   - writes an "Order Cancelled" (or "Damage Verdict") timeline entry
     with a readable description of what happened to the money,
   - for AUCTION orders, puts the product back to 'active' (relisted)
     if it is still 'sold' - the sale fell through. The finished
     auction record itself stays 'completed' as history.
The forfeit split percentage (default 80/20) is configurable via
ESEWA_FORFEIT_SELLER_SHARE (section 15).

11. DATABASE COLUMNS
=====================================================================
Table: orders

  From add_deposit_fields_to_orders_table:
    auction_id                 links the order to its auction (nullable;
                               set to NULL if the auction is deleted)
    deposit_amount             decimal(15,2), nullable - the MINIMUM,
                               fixed at auction-end time
    deposit_status              enum, default 'not_required' (section 9)
    deposit_transaction_uuid   nullable and UNIQUE; the uuid of the

  From add_cancellation_reason_to_orders_table:
    cancellation_reason_category   one of the 5 OrderCancellationReason
                                   values (empty for seller cancels)
    cancellation_note              free text

  From later migrations:
    complaint_status               null / under_review / confirmed_
                                   damaged / not_damaged
    complaint_resolution_note      the admin's note on the verdict

Table: payment_transactions  (the ledger, section 8)
    order_id, type, amount, payment_method (esewa/cash), status
    (pending/completed/failed), party (admin/buyer/seller), reference
    (the eSewa transaction_uuid, when relevant), notes

Table: payout_requests  (section 8)
    order_id, recipient_id, recipient_role (buyer/seller), purpose
    (seller_forfeit_share/buyer_refund), amount, debt_deducted,
    esewa_name, esewa_phone, qr_image_path, payout_status
    (awaiting_details/pending/sent/settled), details_submitted_at,
    sent_at

Table: damage_penalties  (section 8a)
    order_id, seller_id, amount, status (pending/paid/expired),
    verdict_recorded_at, due_at, paid_at, expired_at,
    legal_action_flagged, prior_is_auction_allowed, prior_is_seller
    (snapshotted access to restore later), transaction_uuid,
    restored_at, restored_by_admin_id

Table: seller_damage_strikes  (section 8a)
    seller_id, order_id, damage_penalty_id, amount

Other order columns used here: buyer_id, seller_id, total_amount,
order_number, status, payment_method ('cash_on_meetup' for auction
orders), handover_type ('meetup').

12. CHECKS IN EVERY CONTROLLER METHOD (WHO MAY DO WHAT)
=====================================================================
initiate(Order)   [needs login: route is inside the 'user' group]
   1. The logged-in user must be the order's BUYER, otherwise
      HTTP 403 (forbidden). Sellers and strangers cannot pay someone
      else's order.
   2. acceptsOnlinePayment() must be true (a deposit is pending or
      already partly paid, the order isn't cancelled/completed, and
      there's still a remaining balance). Otherwise the user is
      redirected with a blue message: "No online payment is pending for
      this order."
   3. The chosen amount (?amount=, or the minimum if omitted) must fall
      within allowedRange() (section 8), otherwise a red message
      explains the minimum or maximum.
   4. Otherwise: generate a new uuid, log a pending ledger row, build
      the signed form, show the redirect page.

success(Request)  [public route]
   1. ?data must exist and pass decodeAndVerifyCallback (signature).
   2. The decoded "status" must be exactly "COMPLETE".
      Failing 1 or 2 -> redirect with the red message: "eSewa did not
      confirm the payment. Please try again."
   3. A PENDING PaymentTransaction with that reference (uuid) must
      exist and belong to an order (there is also a legacy fallback for
      payments started before per-payment ledger rows existed, matched
      by the order's stored deposit_transaction_uuid). Otherwise ->
      "Could not match this payment to a pending order."
   4. The callback's total_amount must match the pending transaction's
      amount exactly, otherwise the transaction is marked FAILED and
      the buyer sees "The paid amount did not match this payment."
   5. The status API must confirm COMPLETE (section 7).
   6. Then and only then: mark the transaction COMPLETED, update
      deposit_status, timeline entry, notify seller.

failure(Request)  [public route]
   Redirects to My Orders with the red "cancelled or failed" message.
   It changes nothing in the database - the pending transaction row is
   simply left unmatched and can be retried.

DamagePenaltyPaymentController::initiate/success/failure follow the
same shape, checked against penalty->seller_id and
DamagePenaltyStatus::PENDING instead of order->buyer_id and
acceptsOnlinePayment().

14. NOTIFICATIONS AND MESSAGES
=====================================================================
  DepositPaidNotification -> sent to the SELLER when an online payment
     completes (deposit or any later top-up payment).
     Channels: database (bell menu) + broadcast (real-time).

  SellerDamagePenaltyIssuedNotification -> sent to the SELLER when a
     complaint is confirmed damaged (also a queued
     DamagePenaltyIssuedMail with a direct "Pay Penalty Now" link).
  AdminDamagePenaltyPaidNotification -> sent to every ADMIN when a
     seller pays a damage penalty.
  SellerPermanentlyBannedNotification -> sent to every ADMIN when a
     seller reaches 3 confirmed-damaged strikes.
  AdminLegalActionRequiredNotification -> sent to every ADMIN when a
     penalty goes unpaid past its 7-day deadline.
  Product timeline entries: "Online Payment via eSewa (#ORD-...)" (and
     similar entries for a forfeit, refund or damage verdict) with the

  Toast messages (green success, red error, blue info) come from the
     controllers through the session ("flash") keys esewa_success /
     esewa_error / esewa_info and are shown by OrderDetail and Orders
     when the page loads (or the dashboard, for penalty payments).
  The winner also got AuctionWonNotification + email when the auction
     ended (this is separate from eSewa).

15. SETTINGS (.env / CONFIG) AND GOING TO PRODUCTION
=====================================================================
In .env (read by config/services.php -> 'esewa'):

  ESEWA_PRODUCT_CODE          merchant code (sandbox: eSewa's public test
                              code)
  ESEWA_SECRET_KEY            the signing secret (sandbox: eSewa's public
                              test secret). KEEP THE REAL ONE PRIVATE.
  ESEWA_FORM_URL               eSewa payment page (sandbox:
                              rc-epay.esewa.com.np ... /v2/form)
  ESEWA_STATUS_URL             eSewa status check API (sandbox:
                              rc.esewa.com.np ... /transaction/status/)
  ESEWA_DEPOSIT_PERCENTAGE     minimum-deposit percentage, default 10
  ESEWA_FORFEIT_SELLER_SHARE   the seller's share of a forfeited
                              minimum deposit, default 20 (so the
                              platform keeps the other 80%)
  ESEWA_DAMAGE_PENALTY_PERCENTAGE  the seller's damage penalty, as a
                              percentage of the order total, default 30
  ESEWA_DAMAGE_PENALTY_DAYS    days a seller has to pay a damage
                              penalty before it expires, default 7

Two OLDER config keys still exist in config/services.php but are no
longer read anywhere in the code - ESEWA_SELLER_PENALTY_PERCENTAGE
(default 50) and SELLER_DEBT_OVERDUE_DAYS (default 30). They belonged
to the earlier "50% seller debt" design that the damage-penalty system
above replaced; they're harmless leftovers, not something a fresh
install needs to set.

The defaults in config/services.php are eSewa's PUBLIC sandbox values,
so a fresh clone works with no setup. The sandbox login details for the
test wallet are listed in docs/ESEWA_PAYMENT_INTEGRATION.md.

GOING TO PRODUCTION (not planned for the project, for reference):
   1. Register as an eSewa merchant and get your real product code and
      secret key.
   2. Put the real ESEWA_PRODUCT_CODE and ESEWA_SECRET_KEY in the
      server's .env (never in git).
   3. Change ESEWA_FORM_URL and ESEWA_STATUS_URL to eSewa's production
      addresses (from eSewa's developer documentation).
   4. Use HTTPS, and set APP_URL to your public address so success_url
      and failure_url point to a place eSewa's redirect can reach.
   5. Decide the real percentages, and build the payout submit/mark-
      sent UI (section 8) and a real refund process (the app currently
      only records a PayoutRequest and expects it handled outside the
      app).
   6. Turn APP_DEBUG off. No code change is needed for the gateway
      itself.
After changing .env, restart the server (and run php artisan
config:clear if config is cached).


=====================================================================
16. SECURITY NOTES
=====================================================================
  - The secret key stays on the server; the customer's browser never
    sees it. That is why the customer cannot forge a "paid" reply.
  - The callback is verified with a signature (hash_equals), an amount
    match against our own stored pending transaction, AND an
    independent server-to-server status call using OUR stored amount
    and uuid, never values taken from the browser.
  - Only the buyer of an order can start its deposit payment (403
    otherwise); only the penalised seller can start a penalty payment.
  - Each transaction uuid is unique and matched exactly; an already-
    completed transaction cannot be completed a second time.
  - The database stores only amounts, statuses and uuids. No wallet
    number, password, or card data ever touches our server; the
    customer types them on eSewa's own page.
  - The success/failure routes are public by necessity, but they cannot
    change anything without a correctly signed and confirmed reply.

17. HOW TO TEST IT
=====================================================================
AUTOMATIC TESTS
   php artisan test --compact --filter=EsewaPaymentTest
   php artisan test --compact --filter=OrderPaymentLedgerTest
   php artisan test --compact --filter=DamagePenaltyFlowTest
   They cover:
     - the form signature is a correct HMAC-SHA256 over the documented
       fields in the documented order,
     - a correctly signed callback is accepted and a tampered one is
       rejected,
     - the buyer can open the redirect page for their own order,
       another user cannot start a payment for someone else's order,
     - a callback marks a payment completed only when the status API
       also says COMPLETE and the amount matches,
     - the min/max allowed range and amounts below the minimum being
       rejected,
     - the Group 1 forfeit split, the Group 2 complaint path and both
       verdicts, debt deducted from a forfeit share,
     - the damage-penalty payment flow end to end, strike counting and
       the permanent ban on the third strike,
     - payout details being submitted and marked sent AT THE SERVICE
       LAYER (there is no UI for this yet - section 8).
   (They fake eSewa's responses, so no real network calls happen.)

MANUAL TEST IN THE SANDBOX
   1. Win an auction as a test buyer (or use an existing won auction).
   2. Open My Orders -> that order -> the online-payment card.
   3. Optionally raise the amount box above the minimum, then click
      "Pay via eSewa". You land on eSewa's test payment page.
   4. Log in with the sandbox test account from
      docs/ESEWA_PAYMENT_INTEGRATION.md and confirm the payment.
   5. You return to the order page: the payment shows completed and the
      seller gets a notification.

THE "INSUFFICIENT BALANCE" SITUATION
   eSewa's sandbox test wallet is SHARED by everyone using the public
   test account, and its balance goes down with every test payment. If
   the amount chosen is bigger than the wallet balance, eSewa itself
   says the balance is insufficient. This is not a bug in our code: our
   site sent the correct amount. Ways around it:
     a. Try another test account listed in the docs (each has its own
        balance).
     b. Choose a smaller amount in the amount box (as low as the
        minimum) for demos.
     c. Use a cheaper auction for the demo (a lower winning price gives
        a lower minimum).

USEFUL CHECKS
   - Look in storage/logs/laravel.log for "eSewa callback signature
     verified but status API disagreed" or "amount did not match".
   - The order's transactions() relation (or the money breakdown on the
     order-detail page) shows every ledger row.

18. A FULL WORKED EXAMPLE WITH A TIMELINE
=====================================================================
Auction ends. Sita has the highest valid bid: Rs 32,000. The deposit
percentage is 10%. Ramesh is the seller.

  T+0 s   Scheduler / page view runs determineWinner().
          Auction -> completed. Order ORD-20260915-7KQ2M created:
            total_amount 32000, deposit_amount 3200.00 (the minimum),
            deposit_status 'pending', payment_method cash_on_meetup.
          Sita gets a notification and an email.
  T+2 min Sita opens My Orders -> the order. She sees the minimum
          (Rs. 3,200.00) pre-filled in the amount box, with a note that
          she may pay more if she'd rather not carry cash.
  T+2:10  She raises the amount box to Rs. 10,000 and clicks "Pay via
          eSewa" -> initiate():
            - she is the buyer -> allowed; acceptsOnlinePayment() -> ok
            - Rs. 10,000 is within [3,200, 32,000] -> ok
            - uuid 9b1d... generated; a PENDING payment_transactions
              row for 10,000.00 is logged
            - signature over
              "total_amount=10000.00,transaction_uuid=9b1d...,product_code=EPAYTEST"
            - the redirect page auto-POSTs to eSewa.
  T+2:15  On eSewa's page she pays Rs 10,000.00 with her wallet.
  T+2:30  eSewa redirects her to /payment/esewa/success?data=...
            - signature valid, status COMPLETE
            - the pending transaction is found by uuid 9b1d..., not
              already completed
            - callback amount (10,000.00) matches the pending row
            - our server calls eSewa's status API -> COMPLETE
            - transaction -> COMPLETED, deposit_status -> 'paid'
            - timeline: "Online Payment via eSewa (#ORD-...)"
            - Ramesh receives a notification naming the Rs. 10,000 paid
          Sita sees the green toast and the card now shows Rs. 22,000
          still remaining, payable online again or in cash.
  T+3 days They meet in Kathmandu. Sita inspects the camera and hands
          over the remaining Rs 22,000 in cash. Ramesh marks the order
          completed.

ALTERNATIVE ENDINGS (after some amount was paid online)
  - At the meetup Sita finds the camera is seriously damaged and cancels
    with "The item is damaged". The order goes "under review"; nothing
    moves yet. An admin later inspects it and confirms damage: Sita is
    refunded her Rs. 10,000 in full, Ramesh's account is suspended, and
    he is issued a 30% penalty (Rs. 9,600) due within 7 days.
  - Instead, the admin finds the item was fine (not damaged): the
    complaint is rejected and the Group 1 forfeit rule applies - the
    Rs. 3,200 minimum is forfeited (80% platform / 20% Ramesh) and the
    Rs. 6,800 above the minimum is refunded to Sita.
  - Sita simply changes her mind and cancels with "I no longer want to
    buy this item": the same Group 1 forfeit rule applies immediately,
    no admin review needed.
  - Ramesh cancels for any reason -> Sita is refunded the full Rs 10,000.

If Sita had closed the eSewa page instead of paying, she would land back
on her orders with the red "cancelled or failed" message and could try
again with a fresh amount.

19. THINGS WORTH KNOWING / POSSIBLE IMPROVEMENTS
=====================================================================
These are observations from reading the code, not crashes:

  1. The payout submit-details / mark-sent UI does not exist yet
     (section 8). The service methods and tests are real; the buttons
     on screen are not. A refund or a seller's forfeit share currently
     has to be tracked and paid by looking directly at the
     payout_requests table.
  2. Two config keys (ESEWA_SELLER_PENALTY_PERCENTAGE,
     SELLER_DEBT_OVERDUE_DAYS) and two PaymentTransactionType cases
     (debt_recorded, debt_recovered) are leftovers from an earlier
     "50% seller debt" design that the damage-penalty system replaced.
     They're unused, not wrong, but a bit of noise if you go looking.
  3. Each "Pay via eSewa" click logs its own pending ledger row with a
     NEW uuid. If a buyer opens two eSewa windows for the same click's
     worth of amount, only the one that actually completes is credited;
     the other stays a harmless PENDING row.
  4. The success/failure routes are public. That is required by
     eSewa's redirect, and they cannot mark anything paid without
     valid, confirmed, amount-matched data.
  5. Refunds and payouts are manual outside the app (no eSewa refund
     API is integrated).
  6. Sandbox only: the shared test wallet can run out of balance.

20. SMALL GLOSSARY
=====================================================================
  eSewa            Nepal's online wallet / payment service.
  ePay v2          eSewa's payment-page API used here.
  Sandbox          eSewa's practice environment: fake money, real flow.
  Merchant         The business receiving the payment (our website).
  Product code     Our merchant identifier at eSewa.
  Secret key       The shared password used to create/check signatures.
  Minimum deposit  The smallest online payment allowed on an auction-win
                   order (a configured percentage of the winning price).
  Ledger           The payment_transactions table: every payment,
                   refund, forfeit share and payout is one row in it.
  Payout           A pending transfer the admin owes to a buyer or
                   seller, tracked in payout_requests.
  Damage penalty   A separate 30% fine a seller must pay via eSewa after
                   an admin confirms a buyer's damage complaint.
  transaction_uuid A unique id of one payment attempt; links the eSewa
                   reply to our pending ledger row (or penalty).
  Signature        A code proving a message is genuine and unchanged.
  HMAC-SHA256      The method used to create that signature from the
                   message and the secret key.
  Base64           A way of writing binary/JSON data as plain text.
  Callback         eSewa sending the customer's browser back to us
                   (success_url or failure_url).
  Status API       A direct server-to-server question to eSewa:
                   "did this transaction really complete?".
  Flash message    A one-time message stored in the session and shown as
                   a toast on the next page.
  Refund owed      Status meaning the buyer must be repaid outside the
                   app.
  Forfeited        Status meaning the minimum deposit is split between
                   the platform and the seller.
