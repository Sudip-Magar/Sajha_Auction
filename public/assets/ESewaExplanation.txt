1. WHAT ESEWA IS AND WHY WE USE IT (BIG PICTURE)
=====================================================================
eSewa is Nepal's digital wallet / online payment service. A website can
send a customer to eSewa's payment page, the customer pays with their
eSewa wallet, and eSewa sends the customer back to the website.

IMPORTANT: In Sajha Auction eSewa is used for ONE thing only:
      THE DEPOSIT THAT SECURES AN AUCTION WIN.

Why?  When someone wins an auction there is a real risk that they
simply disappear and never come to the meetup. To discourage that, the
winner pays a small deposit (default 10% of the winning price) online.
The REST (the balance) is paid in CASH at the in-person meetup, after
the buyer has inspected the item.

Everything else stays cash-only BY DESIGN:
  - Second-hand (direct-sell) checkout: in-person meetup + cash on
    meetup / handover. No online payment at all.
  - Auction balance: cash at the meetup.

The integration runs against eSewa's SANDBOX (test environment) by
default: fake money, fake accounts, but the exact same flow as
production. It is meant for the final-year project demo, not for real
money.

3. WHAT HAPPENS STEP BY STEP (THE WHOLE PAYMENT FLOW)
=====================================================================
STEP 0 - The auction ends and the winner gets an order
   determineWinner() creates an Order for the winner with:
     total_amount   = the winning price
     deposit_amount = winning price x deposit percentage / 100
     deposit_status = 'pending'
   The winner is notified (app + email).

STEP 1 - The buyer opens the order
   My Orders -> the order. The "Auction Win Deposit" card shows the
   amount, the status PENDING and the button "Pay Deposit via eSewa".
   (Only the BUYER sees the button, and only while status is pending.)
   The card also explains: "The remaining Rs. X is paid in cash at the
   meetup after you inspect the item."

STEP 2 - initiate  (GET /my-orders/{order}/pay-deposit)
   The controller checks the user is the buyer and a deposit is
   pending (section 12). Then the service:
     - creates a NEW random transaction id (UUID) and SAVES it on the
       order (deposit_transaction_uuid),
     - builds the form fields and signs them (sections 4 and 5).
   The controller shows the redirect page.

STEP 3 - The redirect page auto-submits
   A hidden <form method="POST"> with all the fields is submitted by
   JavaScript to eSewa's payment page (ESEWA_FORM_URL).

STEP 4 - The buyer pays on eSewa
   The buyer logs in with their eSewa account (sandbox test account in
   development), confirms the amount and pays.

STEP 5a - eSewa sends the buyer back: SUCCESS
   eSewa redirects the browser to our success_url with a query
   parameter  ?data=<base64 text>.  Controller success():
     1. decode + verify the signature (section 6)
     2. check status inside the data is "COMPLETE"
     3. find the order by transaction_uuid; refuse if unknown or
        already paid
     4. ask eSewa's status API to confirm (section 7)
     5. only then: deposit_status = 'paid'
     6. write a timeline entry on the product
     7. notify the SELLER (DepositPaidNotification)
     8. redirect to the order page with a green toast:
        "Deposit paid successfully! The seller has been notified."

STEP 5b - eSewa sends the buyer back: FAILURE / CANCELLED
   eSewa redirects to our failure_url (which contains the order id).
   Controller failure() redirects to that order page with a red toast:
   "The eSewa payment was cancelled or failed. You can try again from
   the order page."   Nothing in the database changes; the deposit is
   still 'pending' and the button is still there.

STEP 6 - The meetup
   Buyer and seller meet, the buyer inspects the item, pays the rest
   (balance) in cash, and the seller marks the order completed.

4. THE FORM SENT TO ESEWA (EVERY FIELD)
=====================================================================
Built in EsewaPaymentService::buildPaymentForm(). Sent by POST.

  Field                     Value / meaning
  ------------------------  -----------------------------------------
  amount                    Deposit amount, 2 decimals, e.g. 3200.00
  tax_amount                "0"   (no tax on the deposit)
  product_service_charge    "0"
  product_delivery_charge   "0"
  total_amount              Same as amount (3200.00). This is what the
                            customer pays.
  transaction_uuid          A fresh random UUID for THIS attempt
  product_code              Our merchant code (ESEWA_PRODUCT_CODE;
                            the sandbox uses eSewa's public test code)
  success_url               Our success route (full URL)
  failure_url               Our failure route + ?order=<order id>
  signed_field_names        "total_amount,transaction_uuid,product_code"
                            (which fields are covered by the signature,
                            and in which order)
  signature                 The signature (section 5)

The amount is formatted like  3200.00  (dot, two decimals, no
thousands separator). The SAME formatting must be used when signing and
when calling the status API, otherwise the signatures do not match.


=====================================================================
5. THE SIGNATURE (HMAC-SHA256): HOW IT IS BUILT AND CHECKED
=====================================================================
WHAT IS A SIGNATURE?
   A signature proves two things: (1) the message really comes from
   someone who knows the shared SECRET KEY, and (2) nobody changed the
   message on the way. Our server and eSewa both know the secret key.
   A customer's browser does NOT, so the customer cannot forge it.

BUILDING IT  (private function sign() in EsewaPaymentService)
   1. Take the fields listed in signed_field_names, IN THAT ORDER.
   2. Join them as  name=value  pairs separated by commas:
        total_amount=3200.00,transaction_uuid=<the uuid>,product_code=EPAYTEST
   3. Compute HMAC-SHA256 of that text using the secret key
      (ESEWA_SECRET_KEY), as raw binary.
   4. Base64-encode the result. That text is the "signature".
   In code:  base64_encode( hash_hmac('sha256', $message, $secret, true) )

ORDER MATTERS
   total_amount, then transaction_uuid, then product_code. A different
   order or a different amount format gives a completely different
   signature and eSewa rejects the payment.

CHECKING eSewa'S RESPONSE  (decodeAndVerifyCallback)
   eSewa signs its reply the same way, but with ITS OWN list of fields
   (it tells us which one in the reply's signed_field_names). We:
     1. rebuild the message from the fields it names, in its order,
     2. sign it with OUR secret key,
     3. compare with the signature it sent using hash_equals()
        (a safe comparison that does not leak timing information).
   Different -> the reply is rejected (treated as tampered).


=====================================================================
6. THE CALLBACK: WHAT ESEWA SENDS BACK
=====================================================================
On success eSewa redirects the browser to:
      /payment/esewa/success?data=<BASE64 TEXT>

The base64 text decodes to JSON like this (values are examples):
  {
    "transaction_code": "ABC123",
    "status": "COMPLETE",
    "total_amount": "3200.00",
    "transaction_uuid": "9b1d6c3e-....",
    "product_code": "EPAYTEST",
    "signed_field_names": "transaction_code,status,total_amount,transaction_uuid,product_code",
    "signature": "<base64 signature>"
  }
decodeAndVerifyCallback() returns null (= reject) when:
   - the text is not valid base64/JSON,
   - "signed_field_names" or "signature" is missing,
   - a field named in signed_field_names is missing,
   - the signature does not match.
On acceptance it returns the decoded array.

IMPORTANT: this arrives through the USER'S BROWSER. A person could type
any URL, so we never trust it on its own (next section).


=====================================================================
7. DOUBLE VERIFICATION (SIGNATURE + STATUS API)
=====================================================================
Two independent checks must BOTH pass before the deposit is marked paid:

  CHECK 1 - Signature (section 5/6)
     Proves the reply was created by someone who knows the secret key,
     and was not changed.

  CHECK 2 - eSewa's status API (checkStatus)
     Our SERVER (not the browser) calls eSewa directly:
        GET {ESEWA_STATUS_URL}
            ?product_code=...
            &total_amount=<order's deposit amount, 2 decimals>
            &transaction_uuid=<the uuid saved on the order>
     eSewa answers with JSON containing "status". Only  "COMPLETE"
     passes. If the call fails (not HTTP 200) or the status is anything
     else (for example "PENDING"), the deposit is NOT marked paid, a
     warning is written to laravel.log, and the buyer sees:
        "Payment could not be confirmed with eSewa. If money was
         deducted, contact support."
   Note that the amount and uuid used here come from OUR database (the
   order), not from the browser.


=====================================================================
8. DEPOSIT AMOUNT: HOW IT IS CALCULATED
=====================================================================
In AuctionEngineService::determineWinner(), when the order is created:

      deposit_amount = round( winning_price x percentage / 100 , 2 )

  percentage = config('services.esewa.deposit_percentage')
             = ESEWA_DEPOSIT_PERCENTAGE in .env  (default 10)

  Example:  winning price Rs 32,000  ->  deposit 3,200.00
            balance in cash at the meetup = 32,000 - 3,200 = 28,800

  Example:  winning price Rs 500,000 ->  deposit 50,000.00
            (a big deposit needs a big wallet balance, see section 17)

The amount is computed ONCE, when the auction ends, and stored in the
order. Changing ESEWA_DEPOSIT_PERCENTAGE later only affects NEW
auction wins; existing orders keep their stored deposit_amount.
The order's notes explain it: "A 10% deposit (Rs. 3,200.00) secures the
win via eSewa; the remaining balance is paid in cash at the meetup."


=====================================================================
9. DEPOSIT STATUS VALUES AND EVERY TRANSITION
=====================================================================
Column: orders.deposit_status

  Value          Meaning                              Shown as
  -------------  -----------------------------------  -------------------
  not_required   Default. Ordinary second-hand         (no deposit card)
                 orders have no deposit.
  pending        Auction win; deposit not paid yet.    PENDING (amber)
  paid           eSewa confirmed the payment.          PAID via eSewa (green)
  refund_owed    Paid, then the order was cancelled    REFUND OWED TO BUYER
                 in a way that entitles the buyer      (blue)
                 to the money back.
  forfeited      Paid, then the buyer cancelled        FORFEITED (red)
                 without a good reason; the seller
                 keeps the deposit.

TRANSITIONS
   (order created by an auction win)       -> pending
   pending  --eSewa confirms COMPLETE-->      paid
   pending  --failure/cancel on eSewa-->      pending (unchanged, retry)
   paid     --seller cancels-->               refund_owed
   paid     --buyer cancels: "defect or not as described"--> refund_owed
   paid     --buyer cancels: "changed mind" or "other"-->    forfeited
   pending  --order cancelled-->              pending (unchanged, see
                                              section 19)
   Ordinary second-hand orders stay not_required forever.

The refund itself is NOT automatic: eSewa's refund API is not
integrated. "REFUND OWED" only records that money must be paid back to
the buyer OUTSIDE the app (the order page says so).


=====================================================================
10. CANCELLATION RULES: REFUND OR FORFEIT
=====================================================================
Code: app/Services/OrderCancellationService.php  (cancel())

Cancelling now requires going through the "Cancel Order" form on the
order page (a one-click cancel no longer exists). The buyer must pick a
reason:
   defect_mismatch : "The item had a defect or did not match the listing"
   changed_mind    : "I no longer want to buy this item"
   other           : "Other reason"
An optional note can be added. The seller does not choose a reason.

What happens to a PAID deposit:
   Who cancels        Reason                     Result
   -----------------  -------------------------  ------------
   Seller             (any)                      refund_owed
   Buyer              defect_mismatch            refund_owed
   Buyer              changed_mind               forfeited
   Buyer              other                      forfeited

WHY:  the deposit exists to discourage a winner from backing out for no
reason. If the SELLER is at fault or the item was genuinely not as
described, the buyer gets the deposit back. If the buyer just changed
their mind, the deposit is lost.

Other things cancel() does:
   - saves cancellation_reason_category and cancellation_note,
   - writes a "Order Cancelled" timeline entry with a readable
     description (including what happens to the deposit),
   - for AUCTION orders, puts the product back to 'active' (relisted)
     because the sale fell through. The finished auction record itself
     stays 'completed' as history.
The order page warns the buyer beforehand: "your paid deposit is
refunded if the item had a genuine defect/mismatch, but forfeited if
you simply changed your mind."

12. CHECKS IN EVERY CONTROLLER METHOD (WHO MAY DO WHAT)
=====================================================================
initiate(Order)   [needs login: route is inside the 'user' group]
   1. The logged-in user must be the order's BUYER, otherwise
      HTTP 403 (forbidden). Sellers and strangers cannot pay someone
      else's deposit.
   2. needsDeposit() must be true (status 'pending'). Otherwise the
      user is redirected to the order page with a blue message:
      "No deposit is pending for this order."
   3. Otherwise: generate a new uuid, build the signed form, show the
      redirect page.

success(Request)  [public route]
   1. ?data must exist and pass decodeAndVerifyCallback (signature).
   2. The decoded "status" must be exactly "COMPLETE".
      Failing 1 or 2 -> redirect to My Orders with the red message:
      "eSewa did not confirm the payment. Please try again."
   3. An order with that transaction_uuid must exist and must NOT be
      paid already. Otherwise -> "Could not match this payment to a
      pending order."
   4. The status API must confirm COMPLETE (section 7).
   5. Then and only then: mark paid, timeline entry, notify seller.

failure(Request)  [public route]
   Reads ?order, redirects to that order page (or My Orders if it is
   missing/unknown) with the red "cancelled or failed" message. It
   changes nothing in the database. (The order page itself is protected:
   only the buyer or seller of that order can open it.)

15. SETTINGS (.env / CONFIG) AND GOING TO PRODUCTION
=====================================================================
In .env (read by config/services.php -> 'esewa'):

  ESEWA_PRODUCT_CODE          merchant code (sandbox: eSewa's public test
                              code)
  ESEWA_SECRET_KEY            the signing secret (sandbox: eSewa's public
                              test secret). KEEP THE REAL ONE PRIVATE.
  ESEWA_FORM_URL              eSewa payment page (sandbox:
                              rc-epay.esewa.com.np ... /v2/form)
  ESEWA_STATUS_URL            eSewa status check API (sandbox:
                              rc.esewa.com.np ... /transaction/status/)
  ESEWA_DEPOSIT_PERCENTAGE    deposit percentage, default 10

The defaults in config/services.php are eSewa's PUBLIC sandbox values,
so a fresh clone works with no setup. The sandbox login details for the
test wallet are listed in docs/ESEWA_PAYMENT_INTEGRATION.md.

GOING TO PRODUCTION (not planned for the project, for reference):
   1. Register as an eSewa merchant and get your real product code and
      secret key.
   2. Put the real ESEWA_PRODUCT_CODE and ESEWA_SECRET_KEY in the
      server's .env (never in git).
   3. Change ESEWA_FORM_URL and ESEWA_STATUS_URL to eSewa's production
      addresses (from eSewa's developer documentation).
   4. Use HTTPS, and set APP_URL to your public address so success_url
      and failure_url point to a place eSewa's redirect can reach.
   5. Decide the real deposit percentage, and add a real refund process
      (the app currently only records "refund owed").
   6. Turn APP_DEBUG off. No code change is needed for the gateway
      itself.
After changing .env, restart the server (and run php artisan
config:clear if config is cached).


=====================================================================
16. SECURITY NOTES
=====================================================================
  - The secret key stays on the server; the customer's browser never
    sees it. That is why the customer cannot forge a "paid" reply.
  - The callback is verified with a signature (hash_equals) AND an
    independent server-to-server status call. The status call uses the
    amount and uuid from our own database.
  - Only the buyer of an order can start its payment (403 otherwise).
  - The transaction uuid is unique in the database and matched exactly.
  - An already-paid order cannot be "paid" a second time.
  - The database stores only the deposit amount, status and uuid. No
    wallet number, password, or card data ever touches our server;
    the customer types them on eSewa's own page.
  - The success/failure routes are public by necessity, but they cannot
    change anything without a correctly signed and confirmed reply.

17. HOW TO TEST IT
=====================================================================
AUTOMATIC TESTS
   php artisan test --compact --filter=EsewaPaymentTest
   They cover:
     - the form signature is a correct HMAC-SHA256 over the documented
       fields in the documented order,
     - a correctly signed callback is accepted and a tampered one is
       rejected,
     - the buyer can open the redirect page for their own order,
     - another user cannot start a payment for someone else's order,
     - a callback marks the deposit PAID only when the status API also
       says COMPLETE,
     - a callback does NOT mark it paid when the status API disagrees.
   (They fake eSewa's responses, so no real network calls happen.)
   Cancellation rules are covered in OrderStatusAuthorizationTest.

MANUAL TEST IN THE SANDBOX
   1. Win an auction as a test buyer (or use an existing won auction).
   2. Open My Orders -> that order -> "Auction Win Deposit" card.
   3. Click "Pay Deposit via eSewa". You land on eSewa's test payment
      page.
   4. Log in with the sandbox test account from
      docs/ESEWA_PAYMENT_INTEGRATION.md and confirm the payment.
   5. You return to the order page: deposit shows PAID and the seller
      gets a notification.

THE "INSUFFICIENT BALANCE" SITUATION
   eSewa's sandbox test wallet is SHARED by everyone using the public
   test account, and its balance goes down with every test payment. If
   the deposit (for example Rs 50,000 for a Rs 500,000 winning price) is
   bigger than the wallet balance, eSewa itself says the balance is
   insufficient. This is not a bug in our code: our site sent the
   correct amount. Ways around it:
     a. Try another test account listed in the docs (each has its own
        balance).
     b. Use a smaller deposit for demos: set ESEWA_DEPOSIT_PERCENTAGE to
        a smaller number (this only affects NEW auction wins; an
        existing order keeps its stored deposit_amount, which would have
        to be edited).
     c. Use a cheaper auction for the demo (a lower winning price gives
        a lower deposit).

USEFUL CHECKS
   - Look in storage/logs/laravel.log for "eSewa callback signature
     verified but status API disagreed".
   - The order row shows deposit_status and deposit_transaction_uuid.

18. A FULL WORKED EXAMPLE WITH A TIMELINE
=====================================================================
Auction ends. Sita has the highest valid bid: Rs 32,000. Deposit is
10%. Ramesh is the seller.

  T+0 s   Scheduler / page view runs determineWinner().
          Auction -> completed. Order ORD-20260915-7KQ2M created:
            total_amount 32000, deposit_amount 3200.00,
            deposit_status 'pending', payment_method cash_on_meetup.
          Sita gets a notification and an email.
  T+2 min Sita opens My Orders -> the order. She sees:
            "Auction Win Deposit  Rs. 3,200.00  PENDING"
            "The remaining Rs. 28,800.00 is paid in cash at the meetup."
          and the button "Pay Deposit via eSewa".
  T+2:10  She clicks the button -> initiate():
            - she is the buyer -> allowed; deposit is pending -> ok
            - uuid 9b1d... is saved on the order
            - signature over
              "total_amount=3200.00,transaction_uuid=9b1d...,product_code=EPAYTEST"
            - the redirect page auto-POSTs to eSewa.
  T+2:15  On eSewa's page she pays Rs 3,200.00 with her wallet.
  T+2:30  eSewa redirects her to /payment/esewa/success?data=...
            - signature valid, status COMPLETE
            - order found by uuid 9b1d..., not yet paid
            - our server calls eSewa's status API -> COMPLETE
            - deposit_status = 'paid'
            - timeline: "Deposit Paid via eSewa (#ORD-...)"
            - Ramesh receives "Deposit of Rs. 3200.00 paid via eSewa..."
          Sita sees the green toast and the card now says
          "PAID via eSewa".
  T+3 days They meet in Kathmandu. Sita inspects the camera and hands
          over Rs 28,800 in cash. Ramesh marks the order completed.

ALTERNATIVE ENDINGS (after the deposit was paid)
  - At the meetup Sita finds a serious defect and cancels with the reason
    "The item had a defect or did not match the listing" -> deposit
    becomes REFUND OWED (Ramesh must repay Rs 3,200 outside the app);
    the camera is relisted.
  - Sita simply changes her mind, cancels with "I no longer want to buy
    this item" -> deposit becomes FORFEITED (Ramesh keeps Rs 3,200).
  - Ramesh cancels for any reason -> REFUND OWED.

If Sita had closed the eSewa page instead of paying, she would land on
the order page with the red "cancelled or failed" message and could
press the button again.


=====================================================================
19. THINGS WORTH KNOWING / POSSIBLE IMPROVEMENTS
=====================================================================
These are observations from reading the code, not crashes:

  1. A CANCELLED order whose deposit is still 'pending' can still be
     paid: needsDeposit() only looks at deposit_status, and cancellation
     only changes deposit_status when it was already 'paid'. A buyer
     could pay a deposit on a cancelled order. Suggested fix: also
     require order status not 'cancelled' in needsDeposit()/initiate(),
     and/or set the deposit to a closed state when cancelling a pending
     order.
  2. The column comment in the migration lists
     "not_required, pending, paid, failed", but the code actually uses
     "not_required, pending, paid, refund_owed, forfeited" and never
     writes "failed". The comment is out of date.
  3. Each "Pay Deposit" click creates a NEW transaction uuid and
     overwrites the old one. If a buyer opens two eSewa windows, only the
     one started last can complete; the older one will show "Could not
     match this payment to a pending order".
  4. success() checks the signature and status, and the status API is
     called with our stored amount, but it does not compare the
     callback's own total_amount with the order's deposit_amount. The
     status API check makes this safe in practice.
  5. The success and failure routes are public. That is required by
     eSewa's redirect, and they cannot mark anything paid without valid,
     confirmed data.
  6. Refunds are manual (no eSewa refund API).
  7. Sandbox only: the shared test wallet can run out of balance.

20. SMALL GLOSSARY
=====================================================================
  eSewa            Nepal's online wallet / payment service.
  ePay v2          eSewa's payment-page API used here.
  Sandbox          eSewa's practice environment: fake money, real flow.
  Merchant         The business receiving the payment (our website).
  Product code     Our merchant identifier at eSewa.
  Secret key       The shared password used to create/check signatures.
  Deposit          The small first payment (default 10%) that secures an
                   auction win.
  Balance          The rest of the price, paid in cash at the meetup.
  transaction_uuid A unique id of one payment attempt; links the eSewa
                   reply to our order.
  Signature        A code proving a message is genuine and unchanged.
  HMAC-SHA256      The method used to create that signature from the
                   message and the secret key.
  Base64           A way of writing binary/JSON data as plain text.
  Callback         eSewa sending the customer's browser back to us
                   (success_url or failure_url).
  Status API       A direct server-to-server question to eSewa:
                   "did this transaction really complete?".
  Flash message    A one-time message stored in the session and shown as
                   a toast on the next page.
  Refund owed      Status meaning the buyer must be repaid outside the
                   app.
  Forfeited        Status meaning the seller keeps the deposit.
